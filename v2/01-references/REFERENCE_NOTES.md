## Proposed applications

### 1. Three worlds. One clear decision.

STACK CITY  /  ART DIRECTION RESEARCH  /  05 NOVEMBER 2026

**01 / SKETCHBOOK BOTANICALS** — Expressive line, living specimens, a personal field journal.

**02 / WRIGHT-INSPIRED CITY-STATES** — Terraces, civic identity, and the annual Games.

**03 / SYD MEAD RETRO-FUTURE** — Purposeful machines, expandable businesses, readable signals.

Existing artwork, photographs, objects, and game screenshots only. No generated reference art.

### 2. Give every influence a job.

Observe the source → extract a rule → make a new asset → test the interaction.

**LINE / SPACE** — Schiele + Kollwitz: irregular contour, mass, paper, negative space.

**FORM / COLOR** — Wright + Leyendecker + Neiman: architecture, painted planes, celebration.

**FUNCTION / SIGNAL** — Mead: objects designed inside a working world, not isolated decoration.

Across all three: a starter workspace, an expandable storefront, a tool family, an investment decision, and a persistent hub.

Teen appeal statements are hypotheses to test. Reference games are interaction examples, not evidence of financial-learning outcomes.

### 3. Draw the plant’s character.

Expressionist observation, not polished botanical perfection.

**EGON SCHIELE** — Sunflowers (1911): broken contours, exposed paper, angular leaves, localized washes. Let stems and seed heads carry personality.

**KÄTHE KOLLWITZ** — Suburb (1901): charcoal-like mass, tree rhythm, atmospheric depth. Study the environment; human figures are incidental here.

**TRANSLATE** — Loose contour + quiet paper + one fresh accent. Use generous negative space so a sketch can coexist with a clear interface.

Create: flower silhouettes, a nursery sign, a potting-room sketch, and a seed-packet family.

Teen hypothesis: a collectible journal feels personal and authored.

Borrow the mark-making. Keep the play world inviting rather than copying the sources’ somber mood.

### 4. Real plants, distinct silhouettes.

Physical reference for sketching, prop families, and readable collectibles.

**DAHLIA / RHS** — Layered radial petals and a dense center: a premium flower-head silhouette. Simplify petal groups at gameplay scale.

**COSMOS / RHS** — Open petal ring, thin stems, airy foliage: a contrasting starter specimen. Preserve its gesture before surface detail.

**HAWS / ROWLEY RIPPLE** — Long spout, offset handle, copper body: recognizable tool anatomy and a useful upgrade silhouette.

Create: two plant families, bud/bloom/seed variants, packets, terracotta pots, watering can, snips, labels, and a harvest tray.

These are real flowers and a manufacturer’s product photo. Build an original tool design from the functional anatomy.

### 5. A greenhouse is a working place.

Use real spatial structure; reserve painterly density for the growing areas.

**KEW / TEMPERATE HOUSE** — Structural bays and a central path: repeatable greenhouse modules and clear circulation.

**GREAT DIXTER / LONG BORDER** — Layered planting heights beside a path. Separate walkable space from visual abundance.

**BOTANY MANOR** — A compact potting room groups shelving, tools, and warm task lighting.

Create: starter potting room → repairable greenhouse bay → nursery storefront. Add bench, shelves, tray, seed rack, and a checkout counter.

Architecture and plant structure come from observation. The final rendering can remain sketchy.

### 6. Make the journal do useful work.

UI/UX reference: identification, inventory, discovery, and a visible consequence.

**STRANGE HORTICULTURE** — The book sits beside the collection. Compare an entry with the plant without losing the workspace. Borrow proximity and cross-reference.

**BOTANY MANOR** — The Wildflowers clue sheet groups a few labeled specimens. Borrow the small, legible comparison set.

**STACK CITY APPLICATION** — Select seed → preview cost and growing commitment → reserve funds → confirm. Let players revise before spending.

Create: seed catalogue, specimen detail, inventory tray, cost/reserve preview, and a seasonal goal journal.

Reduce handwriting and paper texture around numbers. Keep text editable; use line art as content, not as the control system.

### 7. A city made of strong horizontal ideas.

Organic architecture as a spatial system, with Greek civic functions.

**FALLINGWATER** — Terraces and sheltering planes relate to the site. Translate that horizontal hierarchy into stepped city districts.

**UNITY TEMPLE** — Grouped volumes and repeated bays give a civic building authority without relying on sculpture.

**ROBIE HOUSE / ART GLASS** — Repeated geometry links architecture and small details. Use the same family for windows, signs, stalls, and interface framing.

Create: courtyard workshop, covered market, civic hall, terrace edge, shared garden, and Games pavilion.

Teen hypothesis: a city-state becomes a place to shape and compare.

This is a proposed synthesis, not an assertion that Wright designed Ancient Greek architecture.

### 8. Give the city objects with a purpose.

A covered market and an award vessel connect commerce, identity, and the Games.

**STOA OF ATTALOS** — A reconstructed colonnaded façade: repeatable shop bays, sheltered movement, and a strong street edge.

**PANATHENAIC PRIZE AMPHORA** — Signed by Nikias; attributed to Sikelos, ca. 560–550 BCE. A physical prize vessel gives the Games an object identity.

**ART GLASS TO GRAPHIC SYSTEM** — Translate repeated lines and bounded accent areas into original city emblems, market signs, and category markers.

Create: modular stall, olive-oil jar, prize amphora, bench, shade screen, civic lantern, shop sign, and a supply cart.

Use architecture and useful objects as the identity. Human sculpture is not required; the vessel’s silhouette matters more than its figure decoration.

### 9. Paint the celebration. Keep the plan calm.

LeRoy Neiman for color relationships; J. C. Leyendecker for constructed paint shapes.

**LEROY NEIMAN / BISTRO GARDEN** — Violet shadows, bright warm notes, and repeated color islands animate a social environment. Borrow relationships, not maximum saturation everywhere.

**J. C. LEYENDECKER / 1939 COVER** — The turkey’s grouped feather planes and sharp highlights show how a useful silhouette can be built from deliberate strokes.

**LEROY NEIMAN / BUILDING SKETCH** — Economical marks establish architectural identity. A loose environment sketch can communicate a decisive idea.

Create: painted storefront material study, Games banners, a ceramic reward, and a civic color script.

Use Neiman-like energy in event scenes. Keep budget surfaces quieter; use Leyendecker’s treatment on objects rather than centering characters.

### 10. Compare strategies, not just wealth.

UI/UX reference: city readability, limited toolsets, and visible placement consequences.

**ISLANDERS** — A restrained bottom tool strip leaves the city visible. Object families remain legible through simple form and color.

**DORFROMANTIK** — A small active tile set and clear counters reduce the number of simultaneous decisions.

**STACK CITY APPLICATION** — City overview → investment preview → compare readiness / reserves / capacity → commit → inspect the Games outcome.

Create: district overview, building inspector, two-city comparison, budget allocation, and event outcome screens.

Use authored comparison cities first. These references do not establish multiplayer, a full civilization simulation, or validated learning.

### 11. Design a machine inside a world.

Visual futurism and industrial design: a coherent place with recognizable functions.

**SYD MEAD / FUTURE URBAN ARCHITECTURE** — Large functional masses, service layers, and controlled warm light. Read the infrastructure before the surface detail.

**SYD MEAD / GYRO PODS** — A family of vehicles shares a design logic. Group the cockpit, body, access points, and support systems into a readable product.

**TRANSLATE** — Expandable service pods and transport connect businesses. Aim for an optimistic work world rather than a dense neon dystopia.

Create: cabin workshop, garage/service bay, expandable commercial pod, transport stop, and a shared market hub.

Teen hypothesis: experimentation and gadget customization make growth tangible.

Mead supplies a design method. These references are not a request to reproduce a film franchise.

### 12. Make upgrades visible in the object.

Reference a useful vehicle family and an operating production space.

**GYRO PODS / SYD MEAD** — Stable body logic with different configurations suggests modular upgrades: capacity, access, storage, and attachment points.

**SATISFACTORY / COFFEE STAIN** — Machines, containers, and connections show what a facility does. Extract functional grouping; simplify the industrial density.

**FINANCIAL CHOICE** — Pilot one service pod or buy extra capacity? Show purchase cost, operating commitment, and remaining reserve before the choice.

Create: delivery pod, modular cargo crate, fabrication bench, solar canopy, vendor terminal, and an upgrade connector.

Each prop should reveal its job. Avoid adding gauges and pipes that imply systems the prototype does not support.

### 13. Use color as a signal.

Existing-media reference: ASTRONEER’s bright exploratory spaces.

**ASTRONEER / SYSTEM ERA** — Broad color groups separate terrain, equipment, and resources. Rounded forms and clear accents support an inviting exploration mood.

**DISTINCT FROM CITY-STATES** — Use cool manufactured surfaces, warm utility accents, and strong silhouette gaps. Keep the geometry engineered rather than terraced and masonry-led.

**ATTENTION BUDGET** — Give the selected machine the strongest signal. Fade secondary connections; keep critical budget labels on a stable surface.

Create: a resource-marker family, machine selection state, route signal, and a material/color chart for the service pod.

Bright color is a hypothesis for approachability, not proof of teen preference. The reference is not a financial-literacy game.

### 14. Reveal what changes when you commit.

UI/UX reference: a spatial system tied directly to resource allocation.

**FTL / SUBSET GAMES** — Ship layout, system slots, and resource indicators occupy a stable frame. Borrow the link between a spatial object and its operating limits.

**ASTRONEER** — A visible base makes equipment and expansion concrete. The world can carry part of the interface.

**STACK CITY APPLICATION** — Inspect pod → compare operating plans → preview reserve / capacity → confirm → see the changed module in the world.

Create: pod inspector, capacity comparison, reserve preview, expandable module selector, and a persistent hub.

FTL is dense and combat-focused. Keep the cause-and-effect clarity while reducing the number of controls for this learning task.

### 15. Keep the worlds distinct.

Shared interaction rules; different visual grammars and sources of delight.

**BOTANICALS** — Irregular contour • paper • living silhouettes
Ownership through collecting and tending.
Key screen: seed choice + seasonal reserve.

**CITY-STATES** — Horizontal planes • masonry • painted accents
Ownership through civic identity and the Games.
Key screen: investment + city comparison.

**RETRO-FUTURE** — Engineered volumes • manufactured surfaces • signal color
Ownership through tools and experimentation.
Key screen: module capacity + cash reserve.

Keep shared: clear labels, reversible previews, confirmation, feedback, focus states, and accessible number presentation.

These are direction hypotheses. Validate teen response and task comprehension before choosing a production style.
