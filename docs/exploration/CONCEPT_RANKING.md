## City-States — owner idea

> City-States - base on Ancient Greek architecture but more accessible and less about human sculpture. The player builds their city-state and compares it somehow with other city-states. Maybe they build towards a yearly Olympics.

### Assessment

Ranked #3 overall by the assistant. Especially strong for architecture, world progression, information design, and strategy UI. It is naturally suited to the societal lens; personal and business finance need explicit supporting experiences.

### Proposed world language

Terraces, courtyards, market streets, stepped seating, colonnades, painted plaster, tiled roofs, and useful shared structures. Aim for an inventive inhabited world rather than depending on sculpture or detailed characters.

Different city-states could derive identity from landscape: harbor, cliff terraces, agricultural valley. Architecture and layout should communicate these differences.

### Proposed progression

Small workshop → market neighborhood → thriving city-state → host of the annual Games.

The Games provide changing demand, investment choices, preparation, and a visible celebration. “Annual Games” is the assistant's working description; the exact event name and mechanics are open.

### Three lenses

| Lens | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Personal | Save toward participation while balancing equipment, training, and other goals |
| Business | Supply visitors; choose whether to test demand or expand ahead of the event |
| Societal | Prepare facilities and transport while preserving resources for surprises |

A persistent city-state could host several learning games. This is a proposed platform structure, not a selected architecture or final premise.

### Comparison UX opportunity

Compare strategies rather than simply wealth:

- Visitor capacity.
- Reserves and flexibility.
- Event readiness.
- Business diversity.
- Supply dependencies.

Use authored example cities first to test whether comparison is understandable. Multiplayer is not necessary to prove the interaction.

### Proposed Figma sequence

City overview → Games preparation → business investment preview → city comparison → event outcome and revision.

This creates opportunities to show hierarchy, budgeting, overlays, comparison, feedback, and recovery.

### Scope boundary

One neighborhood, one business, one Games cycle can demonstrate the idea. Avoid assuming a full civilization simulator, historical simulation, or online competitive system is required.
