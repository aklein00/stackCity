## What changes in the design discussion

The initial Stack City planning emphasized a contemporary city, car travel, household errands, budgets, currencies, cosmetics, and hiring help. The existing prototype remains a useful baseline.

The supplied brief broadens the opportunity toward entrepreneurial experimentation, metaphor, and an ambitious persistent universe. The assistant recommended exploring a business-launch loop rather than assuming the original errands loop must lead the pitch. That recommendation is not a confirmed replacement.

The owner clarified that the work should not be compressed into a rapid art test. Milestones should be judged by quality, clarity, and evidence of fit.

## Proposed case-study structure

1. Define a small financial learning question.
2. Explore 2–3 strongly differentiated visual directions.
3. Apply them to comparable environments and a real gameplay interface.
4. Explain rejected alternatives and revisions.
5. Demonstrate a connected interaction in native Figma.
6. Show how the direction extends to new assets and progression.
7. Explain tools, AI involvement, human decisions, and actual elapsed time.

The assistant suggested beginning with a storefront and gameplay screen for each direction, then expanding the finalists to room/garage studies and a platform front door. This is a proposed evaluation sequence, not a mandatory six-image cap or a deadline.

## UI/UX should have its own story

Show flows, alternatives, components, interaction states, and usability decisions. A decorative HUD on concept art is insufficient evidence by itself.

A proposed business-launch flow:

Goal / opportunity → plan resources → preview consequences → launch a pilot → inspect outcome → revise → return to the persistent world.

Useful decision types:

- Small pilot versus larger launch.
- Capacity versus presentation versus product investment.
- Immediate commitment versus keeping resources available.
- Expansion after evidence versus expansion on an assumption.

The learning target is the reason behind the choice, rather than memorizing a particular answer or choosing the cheapest option every time.

## Three financial lenses

| Lens | Example question |
| --- | --- |
| Personal | How much can I commit while protecting another goal? |
| Business | What should I test before expanding? |
| Societal | How do businesses affect shared space, resources, and other participants? |

Use the current game prototype as evidence of prior UX work without implying it already covers all three lenses.

## Proposed cohesion and cognitive-load approach

Keep navigation behavior, typography foundations, currency/progression symbols, feedback language, and accessibility conventions recognizable across experiences. Allow setting, architecture, materials, palette, and some framing to vary.

Use rich establishing views and calmer decision views. Give the active task a clear focal point; reduce competing animation and expose only the information needed for the present choice.

These are design hypotheses to demonstrate and evaluate, not validated claims about all teens.
