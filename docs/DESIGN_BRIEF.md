# Stack City — design brief

## Purpose

Present a game world and UI system compelling enough to demonstrate art direction, environment progression, and a practical Figma workflow to a game director. Teach financial literacy through decisions in context.

The pitch emphasizes environments and UI over characters. The world is the main visual attraction; a car is the player's recognizable presence.

## Confirmed direction

- Name: Stack City.
- Intended audience: ages 13–17.
- City viewed from above, with an elevated three-quarter camera proposed for readable buildings.
- Ambition: a premium, next-generation SimCity-like world.
- Car travel connects learning destinations.
- Learning should unlock visual progression.
- Include hard and soft currency, cosmetic upgrades, a store, and budgeted help.
- Actual game wireframes and a shareable Figma prototype come before WorldFarm wireframes.
- Both projects need separate documentation and Figma deliverables.
- Current output is Markdown. Art will generate images locally.

## Working premise — proposed, awaiting the brief

“Build a life, one good decision at a time.”

The player explores a fictional city, plans a week, pursues a personal goal, and adapts when life changes the plan. A first-year-in-the-city story is a candidate, not a final narrative.

Use an age-appropriate independence sandbox: avoid assuming every teenager owns a car, pays rent, or has a salary. The car and household responsibilities can belong to the fictional simulation. Player-avatar age, employment framing, parental involvement, and school alignment remain open.

A short pitch chapter could center on saving for a music setup, creative project, or city event. The mayor idea is an alternate if the brief shifts toward civic finance. Business taxes belong to a later entrepreneurship chapter rather than the first personal-finance lesson.

## Player promise

Explore an inviting place. Make choices that matter to your goals. Learn to recover from surprises. Personalize your surroundings.

## Design pillars

1. **A city worth exploring.** Districts have strong silhouettes, atmosphere, and useful destinations.
2. **Choices before lectures.** Let players act, see consequences, and try another strategy.
3. **Visible understanding.** Learning unlocks activities and environmental richness.
4. **Personal stakes.** Finite money and time compete with something the player wants.
5. **Clear money.** Household funds, cosmetic coins, premium gems, and learning progress have distinct identities.
6. **Recoverable mistakes.** Explain a shortfall and provide meaningful revision paths.

## Core loop

Plan income and commitments → choose a goal → choose a destination → travel → make a decision → see its budget effect → handle a surprise → reflect → earn progress → return to the city.

Initial sessions should be short and finishable; exact duration requires testing. Travel is a quick transition with destination preview and a skip option. Manual driving is outside the first slice.

## Districts

| District | Destinations | Learning focus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Residential | Home, market, community spaces | Planning, groceries, subscriptions, goals |
| Industrial/services | Utility service center, garage | Bills, usage, maintenance, unexpected costs |
| Downtown | Bank, employment hub | Savings, income, later credit |
| Business expansion | Creative studio, small shop | Side income, expenses, tax reserves |

A utility service center introduces the bill. Later remote payment/autopay can demonstrate increased competence. A visit should teach something rather than become repetitive busywork.

## Sources of fun

- A goal the player selected.
- Opportunity costs between time, money, and convenience.
- Short event stories with several reasonable responses.
- Discoverable neighborhood activities.
- Car, home, and neighborhood customization.
- Matched before/after world reveals.

Avoid rewarding endless grinding, unexplained wealth, or simply choosing the lowest price every time.

## First slice

One small neighborhood with a market, utility destination, home, and garage event. One complete budget loop. Store and hiring branches demonstrate the supporting economy. Three visible environment progression states.

## Deferred

Full driving, multiplayer, complete city simulation, large character roster, real-money transactions, live financial accounts, comprehensive taxes/investing/credit, and a production monetization plan.

## Pitch outline

1. Hero city view and player promise.
2. One session shown on a city route.
3. One financial decision with consequences.
4. Same environment across three progression states.
5. Store, currencies, and budgeted help.
6. Live Figma prototype and component/state structure.
7. Modular production approach and achievable next milestone.

## Open questions

Audience subgroups (13–14 versus 15–17), region/currency, curriculum goals, accessibility requirements, platform/orientation, avatar role, monetization constraints, assessment standards, and scope of the director's brief.
